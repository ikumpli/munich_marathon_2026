General sports-nutrition hints, kept casual and eyeballed — not personalized medical or dietitian advice. Adjust to what actually sits well with your stomach in training. The Long-Run Fueling and Race Day cards use real numbers since those two are worth getting closer to right.
🍚 Daily Fueling — the Everyday Version
  • Rest / easy days: normal portions — a fist-ish of rice, pasta or potato with your meal, nothing special needed.
  • Quality or long-run days: bump it up a notch — an extra slice of bread, a bigger scoop of rice/pasta, or an extra piece of fruit through the day.
  • Big long-run weeks (20k+): lean noticeably more carb-heavy — think an extra potato, more fruit, maybe a carb snack mid-afternoon.
  • Protein: aim for a palm-sized portion (meat, fish, eggs, tofu, legumes) at each main meal.
  • Fat: normal amounts of olive oil, nuts, avocado, oily fish — don't overthink it.
💧 Hydration & Electrolytes
  • Baseline: drink through the day so your pee stays pale — roughly 8–10 glasses, more on hot days.
  • On longer or hotter runs: sip regularly rather than chugging once — a mouthful every 15–20 min.
  • Hot, sweaty sessions: toss in an electrolyte tab or sports drink, especially through summer heat — matters more in Jul/Aug training than on the cooler October race day.
  • After a hard or hot session, drink more than feels like "enough" — until you're properly not thirsty anymore.
🌅 Before a Morning Run
  • Easy runs < 60 min: fine fasted, or just water/coffee — no need to eat.
  • Quality sessions or long runs: eat something small 60–90 min before — a banana, 1–2 slices of toast with honey or jam, a small bowl of oats, or a few rice cakes. Keep it light and low-fat so your stomach isn't working hard.
  • Have a glass of water beforehand, no need to chug.
🔁 After a Run — Recovery Window
  • Within the hour: something with carbs + a bit of protein — chocolate milk, a yogurt with fruit and granola, or rice with eggs or chicken.
  • Don't overthink the exact mix, just cover "some carbs + some protein" and you're set.
  • Rehydrate steadily rather than all at once.
  • Matters most after long runs or hard quality sessions — much less important after a short easy jog.
🌙 Evening / Dinner, Day-to-Day
  • Balanced plate: palm of protein, fist of complex carbs (rice, potatoes, pasta, quinoa), plenty of veg, a thumb of healthy fat.
  • Night before a quality session or long run: lean slightly higher-carb, keep it familiar and easy to digest — not the night to try something new or very spicy/fatty.
  • Avoid heavy, fatty meals within 2–3 h of bedtime for sleep quality; keep hydrating through the evening rather than a big volume right before bed.
🏃‍♂️ Long-Run & In-Run Fueling
  • Runs > 75–90 min: fuel during — start ~30–45 min in, then small doses every 15–20 min.
  • Target ~30–60 g carbs/hour (gels, chews, sports drink), scaling toward 60–90 g/hour for your longest runs (24–30k) if your gut tolerates it.
  • Rule of thumb: nothing new on race day — test every gel/drink/timing in training first, especially on the Sunday long runs.
🏁 Race Day Nutrition — Munich Marathon, Oct 11
3 Days Before
Carb-load to roughly 600–750 g of carbs a day — extra pasta, rice, bread, potatoes, fruit, juice. Keep fat/fiber moderate to avoid GI issues; this is about topping up glycogen, not stuffing yourself.
Race Morning
2–3 h before the gun: familiar, carb-rich, low-fat/fiber meal, around 75–200 g of easy carbs — e.g. white bread/bagel + honey, oatmeal + banana. Optional small gel/banana 15–30 min before start.
During the Race
30–60 g carbs/hour from gels/drink, starting ~30–45 min in — don't wait until you feel low. Sip water/electrolytes at stations per your practiced plan.
After Finishing
Recovery meal (carbs + protein) within an hour, rehydrate steadily, then celebrate — you earned it. 🎉
